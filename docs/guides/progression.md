# Progression

## What happens in play

A new character chooses a class and starting stats. As they defeat monsters, experience can raise their level; skills can improve, and a trainer can promote an eligible character. Hunger, regeneration and death affect the character throughout play.

A class can be more than a name (issue #15): an operator can give it perks, limits and speeds for its skills, HP and SP for each level, and ways into it with requirements, costs and rewards, taken at a trainer or from the player's **Game ▸ Your Class** window. Classic's classes are 1.6's and set none of this, so Classic plays as 1.6 did and shows no Class window.

## Where it lives

- [packages/shared/src/rules/progression/creation.ts](../../packages/shared/src/rules/progression/creation.ts): validates creation choices and builds starting characters.
- [packages/shared/src/rules/progression/progression.ts](../../packages/shared/src/rules/progression/progression.ts): calculates experience, levels, regeneration, hunger and death values.
- [packages/shared/src/rules/progression/classes.ts](../../packages/shared/src/rules/progression/classes.ts): a class as the engine reads it (issue #15): its `inherits` chain, perks, skill caps and training speeds, and its ways in (1.6's `promotion` and its `paths`), with the words the Class window shows.
- [packages/shared/src/rules/progression/promotion.ts](../../packages/shared/src/rules/progression/promotion.ts): parses trainer data and checks a promotion, at a trainer (`promoteClass`) or from the Class window (`promoteFromWindow`).
- [packages/server/src/systems/progression/class-window.ts](../../packages/server/src/systems/progression/class-window.ts): the Class window's view and its Change button, and the note a trainer's list shows beside a class with paths.
- [packages/shared/src/rules/progression/skillgain.ts](../../packages/shared/src/rules/progression/skillgain.ts): calculates skill caps and kill-based skill gains.
- [packages/shared/src/rules/progression/progress.ts](../../packages/shared/src/rules/progression/progress.ts): trains a skill from each source by its progression (`chance` or `xp`), works out XP curves, and writes the advance line.
- [packages/shared/src/rules/progression/preview.ts](../../packages/shared/src/rules/progression/preview.ts): the Control Panel's previews (a chance source's chance at each value, an XP curve's level table and actions, a harvest node's success and yield by skill and tier), worked out with `progress.ts`'s and gathering's own rules.
- [packages/server/src/content/skills-page.ts](../../packages/server/src/content/skills-page.ts) and [gathering-page.ts](../../packages/server/src/content/gathering-page.ts): the Skills and Gathering tabs on the server: their views, saves into the content draft, and a skill's removal.
- [packages/shared/src/rules/progression/medals.ts](../../packages/shared/src/rules/progression/medals.ts): calculates held medals, their stat bonus and the award lines from skills.
- [packages/server/src/systems/progression/vitals.ts](../../packages/server/src/systems/progression/vitals.ts): advances regeneration and hunger and handles death and recovery.
- [packages/server/src/systems/progression/medals.ts](../../packages/server/src/systems/progression/medals.ts): prints a medal's line when a skill reaches 90 in play.

Entry points include `packages/server/src/systems/progression/vitals.ts` (`regenStep`), `packages/server/src/systems/progression/vitals.ts` (`hungerStep`), `packages/server/src/systems/progression/vitals.ts` (`checkDeath`) and `packages/server/src/systems/progression/vitals.ts` (`finishDeath`).

## How it works

Creation validates the name, class and stat choices before producing the starting character. Shared progression rules calculate experience from a kill and check level gains; skill gain uses the fighter, weapon and monster. A trainer’s Bio opens a class list; the server checks the trainer, character and price before charging and applying the promotion, which first applies the class's SPmax floor (`statChanges[].atLeast`) and then raises its granted skills (`skillGrants` in `classes.json`) to a roll of 1 to 10 without lowering any. Each way into a class (`waysInto` in `classes.ts`) is 1.6's `promotion` or a `paths` row; a trainer takes the first way from the buyer's class whose requirements hold, in 1.6's order of checks (the class you are, a way at a trainer, from your class, the requirements, the gold with the `TRN|` price, then a path's items), and a path's refusals name what is missing. A path's grants raise skills to fixed values, raise full HP and SP, give items (refused before anything is taken if the bag has no room) and teach spells. The Class window's **Change** takes only a path whose `where` allows it. Class perks join the faction perks where they are read (`spellPower` in `basePower`, `melee` in `attackPower`, `defence` in `factionRows`, the dodge perks, `regeneration` in `regenStep`), and `hpPerLevel` and `spPerLevel` replace the row's in `checkLevelUp`. A class's skill cap is the ceiling `train` and `addXp` use: with one, a faction cap counts only when the character's own faction sets that perk (`capValue`), so a faction can only lower it; without one, the faction caps hold as in 1.6. A class's training speed divides a chance roll's `n` (`scaledRoll`) and multiplies an XP amount (`scaledXp`); 0 never trains and rolls nothing. Kill experience and the level curve are content (issue #42), the `balance` rows `killXp` and `levels` on the Game Rules page's **Levels and experience** card ([balance.json](../modding.md#balance-balancejson)): `speciesKillXp` takes the species' melee or spell XP (or works it out from its level), `meleeKillXp` adds gear, a monster's fixed XP, the flat bonus and the thresholds, and `xpGained` adjusts it by the levels (Classic: less the killer's level; or fixed, or by the monster's level against the killer's). `levelCost` is the XP a level takes from the curve (Classic: 30, 400, then 400 + 2 × level), counted per level or as a running total (`levelTotal`, `expShown` for the XP bar), with an optional highest level. Each level reached adds the row's HP and SP max (Classic: 1 and 1) without healing, and spare experience carries over, so one kill can raise several levels. Saved characters hold their level and the XP within it; a changed curve keeps both and works out only the XP the level takes (`syncLevel`, at login and on a publish), levelling up any character whose XP now reaches it. Character levels have their own structured curve rather than the skills' formula rows: a character level has no ceiling, while a skill's curve stops at 100. Mage skills share one cap table for kills and casts. Each skill is described in detail in the [skill guides](skills/README.md). Stats are calculated (`statsOf`): the allocated stats plus the current class's `statBonus` (with what it inherits) plus the points of every medal held. Medals are not granted: every skill at 90 or more shows its medal on the bio and adds its points, and both go if the skill drops below 90. Reaching 90 in play prints the medal's line.

The world tick advances hunger and checks for death before the first actor pass advances regeneration. Regeneration's period, amounts and hunger thresholds and the death values are rows of the `balance` dataset, edited on the Control Panel's Game Rules page ([balance.json](../modding.md#balance-balancejson)). Hunger is all content, the `balance` row `hunger`: how often and how far it falls (`hungerTick`), its warnings (`hungerWarning`: the lowest one a fall passes is told, once), what being stuffed refuses (`stuffedRefuses` in `packages/shared/src/rules/inventory/equip.ts`, with an item's `use.whenFull`), the most it can ever be (`clampHunger`: food, Diminish Hunger, a death and a new character stay at or below it, and `hungerTick` brings a saved character above it down on its next frame), its steps on the gear-break roll (`breakOdds` in `packages/shared/src/rules/combat/armour.ts`) and whether players see it (`hungerView`). Food raises hunger and an effect row with a negative amount lowers it. A world that shows hunger sends it in the owner's `vitals` (`VitalsView.hunger`), again only when what it shows changes, and the Stats tab draws it under Mysticism. Regeneration uses a session counter that starts at 1 on a fresh login (a resumed session keeps it) and resets at the end of every period, whatever the hunger. A role with `noHunger` never gets hungry, and one with `invulnerable` loses no HP to starving. An arena defeat restores one health and spell point and chooses an exit on the same map without the normal death penalty. Normal death (`checkDeath`) handles its messages, the drop, a faction head on a PvP map, faction exile and the loss of a rider's vehicle (also when its hull runs out, announced as the airship shot down or the ship sunk instead of the death line), then sets HP and SP to the balance row `death` (Classic: 1 and 1, at most the maximums; `deathVitals`) without touching their maximums; murder consequences pass to the social system, and a death more than the balance row `timings.killCreditFrames` (Classic: 40 frames) after the last hit credits no killer. While a Lightning Assassin game runs every death is penalty-free: no drop, head or exile. When the death lock ends (`finishDeath`) hunger resets to the balance row `hunger.afterDeath` (Classic: 40000) and the character respawns at the selected point; a prisoner who dies revives in the jail and the sentence runs on. Character attributes, skills and vitals are persisted; timers and counters used to advance the current session are distinct from that stored state.

## The editors

Skills and how they rise are content, edited on the Control Panel's [Skills & Classes](../running-a-server.md#the-skills--classes-page) page: a skill's name and place on the Skills page, who starts with it, the promotions that grant it, its progression per source (the chance model or the XP model, with formulas as rows) and its level curve, with previews worked out by `progress.ts` itself (`chanceRolls` and `chanceCounts` are the chance model's guards, `formulaValue` its formulas, `curveTotals` the curves), so a preview says what play does. The [Gathering & Crafting](../running-a-server.md#the-gathering--crafting-page) page edits the harvest nodes that train gathering skills, fishing and farming. Removing a skill clears or repoints each reference to it; characters keep their stored values. The **Classes** tab edits each class in three tabs (About, What it gives, Ways in) with a preview of what players see and the class tree; removing a class characters are in asks which class they move to, and the publish moves them (`applyClass` in `systems/admin/content-world.ts`; a publish that takes out such a class any other way is refused). See [Progression](../modding.md#progression) and [Classes](../modding.md#classes) for the data.

## Differences from 1.6

See the [progression bugs fixed](../differences-from-fso.md#progression). A death never lowers maximum HP or SP (see [the design change](../differences-from-fso.md#a-death-never-lowers-your-maximum-hp-or-sp)), and by default only a wanted character drops gold or an item; operators choose [whose deaths drop](../differences-from-fso.md#operators-choose-whose-deaths-drop-and-by-default-only-a-wanted-characters-do). Classic's classes and promotions are 1.6's, with the promotion bugs fixed; everything [issue #15](https://github.com/jberlyn/second-star-online/issues/15) added is for operators to opt into, so it is not a difference from 1.6. Every new character starts at 20 HP and 20 SP whatever the class, which is [kept on purpose](../differences-from-fso.md#every-new-character-starts-with-20-hit-points-and-20-spell-points). The server calculates character growth and runs vitals. See the [server-side progression changes](../differences-from-fso.md#server-side-progression), and [social](social.md) for faction and medal context.
