# Vehicles

## What happens in play

Classic has three vehicles: the Airship, the Boat and the Battleship. Each is a content row (`houses.json` `vehicles`, issue #44), and a world may add its own: a vehicle that sails (boarded from the shore, like the Boat) or flies (boarded on its pads, like the Airship) over the tiles its row names, with its own picture, rider body, hull and, optionally, the cannon. See [Vehicles](../modding.md#vehicles-housesjson-vehicles) for the fields.

- **Airship:** use an Airship Deed while standing still and facing an empty landing pad to build it. Face it and click it or press Ctrl to board; when you reach an empty landing pad, use Ctrl to park and get off.
- **Boat and Battleship:** use the deed while standing still on the shore and facing open water to build the ship there. Face it from the shore and click it or press Ctrl to board. It sails only on water, at walking pace. To get off, stop beside the shore, face a free tile of land and press Ctrl: you step ashore and the ship stays parked on the water.

Only the owner can board. Hits on a rider wear the vehicle's hull instead of the rider's HP, and the HP bar shows the hull while aboard. A player may own any number of vehicles. Building one is one way: a vehicle cannot be turned back into its deed. Where players get deeds and Cannonballs (shops, quests, monster loot) is up to the server's operator: Classic gives only an Airship Deed, as a quest reward (`quests.json`), and no Boat Deed, Battleship Deed or Cannonball.

- **The Battleship's cannon:** press the attack key aboard a battleship to fire a cannonball straight ahead. Each shot uses a Cannonball from your bag, and the cannon reloads for about 5 seconds. The ball stops at the first player, monster or vehicle it meets, at a tile an airship could not cross, or at the edge of the screen. The boat has no cannon.

## Where it lives

- [packages/shared/src/rules/vehicles/rows.ts](../../packages/shared/src/rules/vehicles/rows.ts): the vehicle rows: a row by name, by rider body or by a parked one's name, the tiles a row crosses (`vehicleCrossesAt`, `vehicleWalkGrid`) and lands on (`vehicleLandsAt`).
- [packages/shared/src/rules/vehicles/vehicles.ts](../../packages/shared/src/rules/vehicles/vehicles.ts): checks deed placement, boarding, ownership, landing pads and getting ashore, and holds the hull rules and lines.
- [packages/shared/src/rules/core/walk.ts](../../packages/shared/src/rules/core/walk.ts): the `airshipBlock` grid (`airshipWalkGrid`), where a cannon shot stops.
- [packages/server/src/systems/vehicles/vehicles.ts](../../packages/server/src/systems/vehicles/vehicles.ts): applies those checks, changes ground items and rider state, and handles death aboard.
- [packages/shared/src/rules/vehicles/cannon.ts](../../packages/shared/src/rules/vehicles/cannon.ts): the cannon's numbers (reload, offset, speed, damage), its ammunition (`cannonballSlot`), the rider's firing frames (`cannonFireFrame`), the shot's FX name, tile and hit box.
- [packages/shared/src/rules/housing/houses.ts](../../packages/shared/src/rules/housing/houses.ts): which vehicle a deed builds (`deedUse`, from the `houses.json` `deeds` rows).

Hull rules (`hullMax`, `hullHitDamage`, `hullRepairPerMinute`, `boardingHull`, `sunkText`) live in the shared vehicles module, reading the full hull from the vehicle's row and the wear and repair from the world's `balance` dataset (edited on the Control Panel's [Houses & Vehicles](../running-a-server.md#the-houses--vehicles-page) page, with each vehicle's row); hits reach the hull in `packages/server/src/systems/spells/collide.ts` (`playerCollision`). Movement aboard is chosen in `packages/server/src/systems/movement/movement.ts` (`walkWorld`) and, for map edges, `walkableFor`. Removing a vehicle with its cleanup is `packages/server/src/content/service.ts` (`usage`, `draftRemoval`) and `packages/server/src/systems/admin/content-world.ts` (`vehicleUsage`, `applyVehicle`).

Entry points include `packages/server/src/systems/vehicles/vehicles.ts` (`vehicleDeed`, `vehicleClick`, `unboardVehicle` and `vehicleDeath`); a deed's Use and Ctrl reach them from `packages/server/src/systems/inventory/items.ts` (`useIntent`, `actionIntent`). The cannon fires from `packages/server/src/systems/combat/melee.ts` (`pollAttack`, `pollCannon`) through `packages/server/src/systems/spells/cast.ts` (`fireCannonball`), and its shots are moved by `packages/shared/src/rules/spells/spellfx.ts` (`stepFx`) and stopped by `packages/server/src/systems/spells/collide.ts` (`cannonballHits`).

## How it works

A deed builds the vehicle its `deeds` row names (`deedUse`); one naming a vehicle no row has builds nothing, silently, and is kept. Building checks whether the player is moving, whether the faced tile is on the map, and whether it suits the vehicle: one of its `lands` tiles for a `pad` vehicle (a J2 dock for the Airship), or a tile it crosses for a `shore` vehicle (water for a Boat or Battleship). It must hold no item (and, for a ship, no player). Success consumes a deed, is written to the activity log and creates an owned ground item named for the builder and the vehicle's row, `<name>'s Airship`, `'s Boat` or `'s Battleship`, which blocks the way like any fixture. A stationary owner facing it can board it; another player cannot. Any other click, including one from aboard a vehicle, says whose it is. An item without a stored owner falls back to the name on the item. Boarding removes the ground item, sets the rider's body (the row's `rider`: 57 Airship, 55 Boat, 56 Battleship) and moves the rider onto its tile.

A rider crosses the tiles its row's `moves` names: `over` less `except`, a flag read on the layer its tiles live on, a code on either layer. An Airship (a `pad` vehicle) crosses everything but layer-2 `airshipBlock` tiles, flies over players, monsters, items, exits and webs, and each step takes half a walker's time (`STEP_FRAMES / 2`). A ship (a `shore` vehicle) crosses water only: layer-1 tiles flagged `sea`, whatever shoreline art layer 2 draws over them. It steps at walking pace, is blocked like a walker by players (other than flying riders), monsters and blocking items, parked ships included, and stands in a walker's way (monsters pass riders, as before). Crossing a map edge needs the wrapped tile to be one the rider's vehicle crosses on the next map, so a ship crosses only onto water.

Riders cannot cast or swing, and neither monster nor player melee hits them. Arrows and harmful spells do, but they wear the vehicle's hull instead of the rider's HP: a fixed 2 per hit, whatever the attacker's power. The 2 is the `balance` row `vehicles.hullHit`. The rider's HP bar shows the hull while aboard. A new vehicle starts with its row's full `hull` (Boat 60, Airship 80, Battleship 150); a parked one keeps its hull and regains `vehicles.repairPerMinute` (Classic: 1) for each whole minute parked, worked out when it is boarded and never past the full hull.

To get off an Airship (a `pad` vehicle), the rider must be stationary on one of its `lands` tiles (a J2 pad) without a ground item; the airship parks under them. To get off a ship, the rider must be stationary and face a tile a walker could step onto: walkable on foot, not an exit, and free of players, monsters and blocking items. The ship parks on the rider's water tile, which must hold no item, and the rider steps ashore. Parking restores the player's normal body. A ship keeps the facing it was built with (out to sea) or left with (`ground_items.facing`), and boarding turns the rider that way. The client draws a parked ship (a `shore` vehicle whose row `bobs`; any other parked vehicle shows its `picture`) as its rider's frame for that facing on the riders' swell clock (east, the docked picture on the same pixels, when none is stored), so it bobs at its mooring and boarding moves nothing. The server marks the relevant ground, inventory and vehicle state for persistence together.

A vehicle a content change takes out is [removed with its cleanup](../modding.md#vehicles-housesjson-vehicles): its riders land where they are and its parked ones become their deed, or both switch to another vehicle. Taking out one that is ridden or parked any other way is refused until that cleanup is in the draft. One no row has (a release pack that dropped it, a database edit) rides and parks as nothing: its riders land where they are, logged, at the next tick if online and when they next log in otherwise.

A death aboard, including the hull running out, loses the vehicle and drops nothing but a faction head, [kept on purpose](../differences-from-fso.md#dying-aboard-an-airship-loses-it) as the risk of travelling into danger. A hull running out counts as a kill for whoever hit it last, and everyone reads that the airship was shot down, the ship sunk or, for a `shore` vehicle that does not go on the sea, the vehicle wrecked (`sunkText`), instead of the death line. Vehicles stay out of arenas: a rider cannot challenge, and over arena tiles it is judged by the map's PvP flag alone, dies a normal death and cannot quit. Ctrl aboard first grabs a Pig in front, otherwise takes or uses the item there as on foot, and unboards when there is neither. Nothing can be dropped from a vehicle (items, gold, crystals or a Pig): "You can't drop this from the sky!" on an Airship (any flying vehicle), "You can't drop this into the ocean!" on a ship that goes on the sea, and "You can't drop this from your <vehicle>!" on any other. Items, gold and crystals can still be given to the player in front, and gold to an NPC.

The cannon (a vehicle row with `cannon`: Classic's Battleship) answers each press of the attack key once (`cannonArmed`), on a map where fighting is on; a rider is never in an arena, so arena tiles do not help on a no-fight map. It needs the reload counter (`cannonReload`, set to the `balance` row `cannon.reloadFrames`: Classic 100 frames) at 0 and the world's cannon ammunition (`bindings.json` `cannon.ammo`: Classic's `Cannonball`) in the bag, the first of which it spends (logged as a use); with none it says "You have no cannonballs!" once a press, and a world that binds no ammunition has a cannon that never fires. It then queues the `CannonballShot<dir>` FX owned by the rider. Every client places it 48 px from the rider along the facing, plays `cannonball` on channel 4 and shows the rider's `56-fire1..3` frames (`CANNON_FIRE_ANIM`; a rider body with no firing frames, such as an uploaded one, keeps its standing frame). The shot moves 5 px each `stepFrame` call and stops over a layer-2 `airshipBlock` tile (one the Airship cannot cross), or at the playfield's edge, on the server and on every client alike; if a tile between the ship and where the shot appears is off that grid, it stops at once. After each FX step the server tests the stretch each shot's shadow has crossed since its last test (`cannonballSweep`, starting at the edge of the rider's tile, so something right beside the ship is not stepped over) against players, then monsters, then parked vehicles by tile, and stops it at the first, telling clients with `fxend`. A player takes the `balance` row `cannon.damage` (Classic: 14 to 16, `cannonDamage`) off the hull aboard or off HP on foot, without armour, unless the map's PvP is off, the player is the shooter, dead or loading; a monster takes the same, with the spell death check's loot and XP. A parked vehicle is not harmed.

## Differences from 1.6

Landing pad checks read layer 1 across all 12 rows, fixing the original's row 10–12 lookup. Boats and Battleships, written but switched off in 1.6, are built, with a water-only walk grid 1.6 never had. Vehicles are converted by the server and the original ground item cap is removed. Hulls and keeping vehicles out of arenas are SSO additions. The Battleship's cannon, drawn and placed but never fired in 1.6, fires, with ammunition, a reload and fixed damage ([the cannon](../differences-from-fso.md#the-battleships-cannon-fires)). See the [vehicle bugs fixed](../differences-from-fso.md#vehicles), the challenge fix among the [progression bugs fixed](../differences-from-fso.md#progression), [server-side vehicle changes](../differences-from-fso.md#server-side-vehicles) and [movement bugs fixed](../differences-from-fso.md#movement).
