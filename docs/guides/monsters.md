# Monsters

## What happens in play

A monster can appear on a map, move toward a player, attack and leave loot when defeated. Which monsters can appear depends on the map's roster, while active monsters occupy slots in that roster.

## How it works

A map has four roster slots, each holding a roster entry and at most one live monster. During an actor pass, eligible players make spawn passes against that one shared set of slots. Population changes the gate odds; emulated spawn and mover rolls are limited to at most 15 players, while attacks test each player. Loading, dying (`dontHitMeAgain`) and the delay after entry can prevent a player's pass. An empty slot still needs its pause at 0 (set to 30 on a spawn or a move pick and to 90 on each step), a valid tile, its own roll, any NPC gate and the species' time gate (`spawnMinute`). The tile is drawn at random and refused (`refusesTile`) when it is not walkable (not water, for a sea creature), holds a blocking ground item or, for a three-wide creature, when any footprint cell does; a monster appears facing a random way with its species' hit points. A faction map can substitute its faction's `member` species in the roster for a pass. The gate odds, the move pick, a walk's length, each slot's roll, the faction slot pick and the NPC gate are the `monsters` balance row (the Game Rules page's Monsters card; Classic keeps 1.6's numbers).

Mover selection also uses population and eligible players. A selected monster either draws a random walk or, when it is hostile (a whole entry of the World Editor's monster list, or `rules.hostile`), chases that player, choosing each step toward where they stand as the step starts. Its steps run back to back, 16 frames each, and blocked steps are skipped at no cost in time. Collision uses the map and, for large creatures, a footprint; players and other monsters block a step too, and a blocking ground item ends the remaining walk. Sea creatures (`rules.sea`) spawn and move only on water. Attacks are tested against players: once a pass, each monster rolls the balance row `combat.monsterMeleeOneIn` (Classic: 1 in 10) for each player it reaches. A monster melees only on a map where fighting is on, except an Officer facing a wanted player; a trap (the Web's `trap` flag) or a name with no attack entry never attacks, a `FLG|Passive` NPC attacks only once provoked, a Holy Shield never hits an innocent player, and a rider, a role with `invulnerable` or a player still blinking from a hit takes no blow. The world tick coordinates walking, attacks and spell collisions. Combat and spell handlers apply hits to shared monster health. A monster dies below zero health; a player's killing hit, melee or spell, determines the experience path, sends any kill broadcast (such as the Overlord's) and the server rolls its loot; only a melee kill rolls weapon skill gain. A spell kill gives no experience or loot if its player owner is no longer on the map, or if the caster was a monster or server hazard. Slot state is saved on spawn and death and periodically for health and position. A monster with no NPC data never moves, nor does an `immobile` species (Classic: the Spikes, Web and Holy Shield). The mechanics 1.6 tied to body numbers are species `flags` in `monsters.json` (see the [modding guide](../modding.md#species-flags)). NPC data controls properties such as stillness and chatter, and also supplies shops, biographies, spells and loot. Clicking an NPC whose data has something to show opens its Bio from anywhere on the screen; `unclickable` species and faction members take their own click paths, and clicking a `grabbable` species (Classic: a Pig) on your faced tile, or pressing Ctrl facing it, grabs it as its item, at most once every `timings.pigGrabFrames` (Classic: 500 frames) and not into a full bag. Gold offered from the gold dialog goes to a monster or NPC standing one tile ahead, else two (`goldTarget`; the [economy guide](economy.md) has what the NPC does with it). An NPC with `RND|` lines says one, picked at random, to the whole map every `npcChatter` seconds (a server setting, 150 by default), counted from its spawn; players who hid NPC chatter in their settings do not hear it. Every species has hit points in `monsters.json` (1 where 1.6 gave none); one with no attack entry never melees. A species may have a level (issue #42): kill XP can compare it with the killer's level, a species with no XP of its own takes its kill XP from it (the `balance` row `killXp`; a species row's own `killXp` is a fixed amount, and the old name-matched `rules.killXp` table is gone: see the [progression guide](progression.md)), and when the Game Rules page shows monster levels the server sends it with the monster (`MonsterView.level`, and the NPC Bio's `level`) so the hover tip and Bio title read "Wolf (level 3)" (`shownMonsterLevel`, `withMonsterLevel`). Classic sets no levels and never shows them, as 1.6.

Operators edit monsters on the Control Panel's [Creatures & NPCs](../running-a-server.md#the-creatures--npcs-page) page. It writes a monster's species record and its **definition** (`monsters.definitions`): what the name-matched monster tables decide for it, which the content store compiles into exact rows that win in every table they cover, as an item definition does (see [Monster definitions](../modding.md#monster-definitions)). A defined monster's kill skill cap is by species, ahead of the by-body rows. Its loot, spells and NPC flags stay in its NPC file and save live, as the Talk & Shops tab saves the whole file; every NPC file save names the revision it started from, so Talk & Shops and the Creatures tab never write over each other's newer change.
